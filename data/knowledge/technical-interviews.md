# Technical / coding interviews

Group: Interviews · Related vault file: `Job/Interview Prep/Prep Plan.md` · 7 sources, 12 findings

## Findings (strongest sources first)

- Anthropic does not allow AI during live interviews unless told otherwise. (Anthropic, 2025; high)
  - Takeaway: Default to no AI in live rounds unless the company says AI is allowed.
- Canva expects backend, ML and frontend candidates to use AI tools like Copilot, Cursor and Claude in technical interviews. (Canva Engineering Blog, 2025-06-11; high)
- Canva uses open-ended realistic problems that can't be solved with one prompt and rewards clarifying requirements and critically reviewing AI code. **example: "Build a control system for managing aircraft takeoffs and landings at a busy airport"** (Canva Engineering Blog, 2025-06-11; high)
  - Takeaway: Ask clarifying questions first and show you catch AI mistakes.
- Candidates secretly using ChatGPT passed most interviews built on verbatim or slightly modified LeetCode questions but few with custom questions. **73% verbatim LeetCode, 67% modified, 25% custom; 53% platform baseline; 32 usable candidates** (interviewing.io, n.d.; high)
  - Takeaway: Expect more custom, follow-up-heavy questions; memorizing LeetCode solutions is worth less than real problem solving.
- No interviewer in the experiment flagged any candidate as cheating. **"Not a single interviewer mentioned concerns about any of the candidates cheating"** (interviewing.io, n.d.; high)
  - Takeaway: This finding is why companies are moving to in-person or AI-allowed formats; prepare for both.
- In a survey of interviewers, most at Big Tech said they had suspected candidates of using AI to cheat, and about a third had caught someone. **81% suspected; 31% caught; 63 interviewer respondents from interviewing.io** (The Pragmatic Engineer, 2025; medium)
  - Takeaway: Never use hidden AI in a no-AI round; the reputational risk is real.
- In the pilot, candidates with little AI experience struggled mostly because they couldn't guide the AI or spot weak suggestions. (Canva Engineering Blog, 2025-06-11; medium)
- Big Tech is using tougher questions while startups are dropping algorithmic questions and take-homes. (The Pragmatic Engineer, 2025; medium)
  - Takeaway: Tailor prep by target: LeetCode depth for Big Tech, practical build/debug for startups.
- Junior and entry-level hiring grew much more slowly than senior hiring. **lead +22%, senior +19%, junior +9%, entry-level +7% YoY; 74% of developers struggle to land jobs** (HackerRank, 2025; medium)
- Developers prefer practical challenges and say assessments don't match real work. **66% prefer practical challenges; 78% say assessments don't align with real tasks; survey of 13,732 respondents in 102 countries** (HackerRank, 2025; medium)
- The AI-enabled round is graded on problem solving, code quality, verification, and communication. **"Should use AI, but need to show you understand the code... Don't prompt your way out of it."** (Hello Interview, 2025-10; medium)
  - Takeaway: Narrate, test AI output, and never paste code you can't explain.
- Meta's AI-enabled round replaces one of two onsite coding rounds; the other stays a traditional no-AI round. **60 minutes in CoderPad with built-in AI assistant (per secondary reports); reported for E5-E7 and M2** (Hello Interview, 2025-10; medium)
  - Takeaway: Still drill classic algorithms, and separately practice coding with an AI assistant in an unfamiliar codebase.

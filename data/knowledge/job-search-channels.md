# Job search channels

Group: Search · Related vault file: `Job/Playbook/Where to Find Jobs.md` · 7 sources, 13 findings

## Findings (strongest sources first)

- External job boards bring in half of all applications but under a quarter of hires. **External job boards: 49.9% of applications, 22.6% of hires (2025); 17% of hires in 2024** (Greenhouse, 2026-03; high)
  - Takeaway: Mass-applying on job boards is the lowest-yield channel; cap time spent there.
- Company career sites (company job boards) are the top source of hires, producing a bigger share of hires than of applications. **Company job boards: 23.8% of applications, 34.4% of hires (2025)** (Greenhouse, 2026-03; high)
  - Takeaway: Apply directly on company career pages rather than through aggregators when possible.
- Tech roles lean more on sourcing than business roles. **Tech jobs: 24% of hires from sourcing vs 18% for business roles** (Ashby (Talent Trends Report), 2025-10-17; high)
  - Takeaway: As an engineer, being sourceable (LinkedIn, GitHub) matters more than for non-tech roles.
- Applications per job roughly doubled from 2022 to 2025 across 6,000+ companies and 640M+ applications. **116 (2022) to 244 (2025) applications per job, +111%** (Greenhouse, 2026-03; high)
  - Takeaway: Each posting is crowded; a generic application is one of ~240.
- Inbound applications were the source of about half of all hires in Q2 2025, a four-year high, in a dataset of ~250,000 hires. **Inbound 52% of hires; sourced ~16%; referral ~18% (Q2 2025)** (Ashby (Talent Trends Report), 2025-10-17; high)
  - Takeaway: Applying still works at startups on Ashby; do it well and fast rather than skipping it.
- Standard/junior tech postings fell more than senior ones versus 2020. **Senior roles down 19%, standard roles down 34% vs 2020** (Indeed Hiring Lab, 2025-07-30; high)
  - Takeaway: Early-career competition is heavier; warm channels matter more.
- Recruiter headcount per organization fell sharply while each recruiter handles about three times as many applications. **Recruiters per org 10.43 (2022) to 4.62 (2025); applications per recruiter 1,610 to 4,890** (Greenhouse, 2026-03; high)
  - Takeaway: Recruiters skim fast; the first lines of the resume and profile must carry the match.
- Time to fill got longer. **43.64 days (2022) to 59.67 days (2025), +37%** (Greenhouse, 2026-03; high)
  - Takeaway: Plan for roughly two months from application to hire per role.
- The share of US tech postings asking for 5+ years of experience rose while 2-4 year roles shrank. **5+ yrs: 37% (Q2 2022) to 42% (Q2 2025); 2-4 yrs: 46% to 40%; 1 yr or less: steady at 17-18%** (Indeed Hiring Lab, 2025-07-30; high)
- Response rates differ a lot by job platform in Huntr users' 1.7M tracked applications; LinkedIn and Dice were near the bottom. **Google Jobs 11.3%, Wellfound 6.0%, Glassdoor 5.5%, Indeed 4.5%, LinkedIn 3.1%, Dice 0.35%** (Huntr, 2026; medium)
  - Takeaway: Use LinkedIn to find roles, then apply on the company site; Wellfound is decent for startups.
- In a survey of ~500 mostly experienced engineers, warm referrals ranked as the most effective channel, followed by in-house recruiters reaching out, applying online, and cold outreach to hiring managers. **~500 respondents, +1/-1 ranking** (interviewing.io, n.d.; medium)
  - Takeaway: Order of effort: warm referrals, being found by in-house recruiters, then targeted applications.
- Tailored resumes converted to interviews/offers about 1.6x better than untailored ones. **5.8% vs 3.73%** (Huntr, 2026; medium)
  - Takeaway: Tailor the resume per role (the quick-bid workflow) instead of one generic version.
- Applications needed per offer are highly spread out. **38% got an offer within 30 applications; 18% needed over 100** (Huntr, 2026; medium)
  - Takeaway: Budget for 30-100+ applications and track conversion weekly.

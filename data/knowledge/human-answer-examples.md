# Human answer examples

Group: Writing · Related vault file: `Job/Writing/04-question-playbook.md` · 6 sources, 10 findings

## Findings (strongest sources first)

- Example: "Competing products work at the wrong layer of abstraction and/or force the user to constantly think and do things." Worked because it's an opinionated, specific diagnosis, not a feature list. (Y Combinator, 2007; high)
  - Takeaway: When asked about a technical choice, name the tradeoff plainly.
- Example: people's file names like 'proposal v2 good revised NEW 11-15-06.doc' "continue to crack me up." Worked because a small real detail plus a bit of personality makes the pain obvious and the writer human. (Y Combinator, 2007; high)
  - Takeaway: One vivid real detail (e.g. a Burmese-language question answered from a scanned diagram) beats adjectives.
- Example: "Dropbox synchronizes files across your/your team's computers. It's much better than uploading or email, because it's automatic..." Worked because sentence one says exactly what it does and why it beats the alternative. (Y Combinator, 2007; high)
  - Takeaway: Describe a project in one plain sentence: what it does, then why it's better than the obvious workaround.
- A reader applied to only three jobs, wrote a slightly better tailored letter each time, and got a fast interview call with the last; the hiring manager called the letter a marker of applicant quality. (Ask a Manager, 2020-10; medium)
  - Takeaway: Fewer, tailored applications beat mass applying.
- Green's examples favor small concrete actions over adjectives, e.g. a candidate who "advised my co-op on how to secure garbage cans" to show real expertise. (Ask a Manager, 2020-10; low)
- Structure that works: common ground, proof you're worth talking to, a light call to action like "Would you be up for a quick chat this week or next?"; don't open with how you found them, don't attach a resume, don't lead with the job. (interviewing.io, n.d.; medium)
  - Takeaway: Short message, first names, one accomplishment, low-pressure ask.
- Recommended opener: "I've read your work on {topic}, and I {your thoughts}." Works because it starts with common ground and a real opinion, not the job. **Personalized template reply rate 25-50% vs generic 5-25%** (interviewing.io, n.d.; medium)
  - Takeaway: Open recruiter/HM messages with a real reaction to something they built or wrote, then one proof line.
- The test is that the application shouldn't look mass-sent. **"You don't want your application to look like it was just randomly shotgunned out."** (Hacker News, 2025-07-10; medium)
  - Takeaway: Include at least one detail that only fits this company.
- Commenters valued narratives the resume can't carry, like side projects or career pivots; a former recruiter preferred a two-paragraph summary over scanning a CV. (Hacker News, 2024-12-28; low)
- A reply argued even minimal personalization like "I want to work in Scala" is enough to show it isn't a mass application; another noted this matters most at early-stage startups. (Hacker News, 2016-05-23; low)
  - Takeaway: At startups, one honest specific line ('I want to work on retrieval quality in production') carries a lot.

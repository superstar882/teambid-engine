# Early-career SWE market

Group: Market · Related vault file: `Job/Profile/Candidate Profile.md` · 9 sources, 15 findings

## Findings (strongest sources first)

- The decline comes through reduced hiring, and is concentrated where AI automates work rather than augments it. (Stanford Digital Economy Lab, 2025-08; high)
  - Takeaway: Target roles that build or apply AI (augmenting) rather than routine coding roles.
- Tech postings overall remain well below pre-pandemic levels while AI-mentioning postings are well above them. **Tech postings 34% below Feb 2020; AI-mentioning postings 45% above Feb 2020** (Indeed Hiring Lab, 2026-01-22; high)
  - Takeaway: Generic SWE applications face a shrunken pool; AI-tagged roles are where openings are.
- Recent college graduates (ages 22-27) have elevated unemployment and high underemployment as of 2026 Q2. **Unemployment about 5.6%; underemployment edged up to 42%** (Federal Reserve Bank of New York, 2026; high)
- Workers aged 22-25 in AI-exposed occupations have employment well below expected levels versus less-exposed peers, while experienced workers show no such gap (latest revision Aug 2026). **19% below expected levels** (Stanford Digital Economy Lab, 2025-08; high)
  - Takeaway: Early-career status is the risk factor, so frame experience by what was shipped, not by age or grad year.
- Coverage of the original paper reported that employment of software developers aged 22-25 fell nearly 20% from its late-2022 peak. **nearly 20% decline from late-2022 peak** (Stanford Digital Economy Lab, 2025-08; medium)
- Secondary coverage of the NY Fed's by-major table reports computer science and computer engineering among the higher-unemployment majors for recent grads (not visible on the fetched page itself). **CS about 7.0%, computer engineering about 7.8% (secondary sources)** (Federal Reserve Bank of New York, 2026; low)
  - Takeaway: A CS degree alone is not a hiring advantage right now.
- Software engineering fell from a consistent top-5 role to ninth among most-posted roles on Handshake in 2024-25. **ninth among most-posted roles** (Handshake, 2025-08-14; high)
- New grads are a small and shrinking share of Big Tech hires. **7% of Big Tech hires; down 25% from 2023 and over 50% from 2019** (SignalFire, 2025-05-20; high)
- At startups, new grads are also a small share of hires and that share has fallen. **Under 6% of startup hires; down 11% from 2023 and over 30% from 2019** (SignalFire, 2025-05-20; high)
- CS majors are a growing share of graduates while sentiment has turned negative. **CS rose from 7% of Class of 2020 to 11% of Class of 2026; 70% of Class of 2026 CS majors express some pessimism** (Handshake, 2025-08-14; high)
  - Takeaway: More competition from peers, so differentiation through real work matters more.
- Recent grads made up a much smaller share of engineering hires at larger companies in 2025 than in 2023. **about 1 in 10 hires in 2025 vs nearly 3 in 10 in 2023** (The Pragmatic Engineer, 2025-09; medium)
- AI use among developers is nearly universal and a large share of code is AI-generated. **97% use at least one AI assistant; 29% of code AI-generated on average (13,732 respondents)** (HackerRank, 2025; medium)
  - Takeaway: Interviewers assume AI fluency; the edge is judgment and debugging.
- Explicitly entry-level postings are a small share of software engineer listings. **4.0% (1,915 of 48,207); median US base $140,000** (InterviewStack.io, 2026-05-20; medium)
- A related Robert Half survey found most HR leaders say reviewing AI-generated applications slows hiring. **67% of US HR leaders; 20% cite delays over two weeks** (Robert Half (via PR Newswire), 2026-03; medium)
  - Takeaway: Expect slower processes; follow up and use referrals.
- Hiring signals grew faster for senior roles than for junior and entry-level roles. **Entry-level +7%, junior +9% vs 19-22% for senior roles** (HackerRank, 2025; medium)

# Plain language

Group: Writing · Related vault file: `Job/Writing/03-plain-words.md` · 5 sources, 13 findings

## Findings (strongest sources first)

- Shorter sentences help with complex information, and long tangled sentences signal unclear thinking. **"Long, complicated sentences often mean that you aren't sure about what you want to say."** (digital.gov (U.S. General Services Administration), n.d.; high)
  - Takeaway: Split any sentence with more than one dependent clause.
- Keep paragraphs short and on one topic. **"paragraphs of no more than 150 words in three to eight sentences"; "Paragraphs should never be longer than 250 words."** (digital.gov (U.S. General Services Administration), n.d.; high)
  - Takeaway: Hard cap: 150 words per paragraph; for proposals far less.
- Use active voice so it's clear who does what. **"Active voice makes it clear who should do what."** (digital.gov (U.S. General Services Administration; successor to plainlanguage.gov), n.d.; high)
  - Takeaway: Write 'I built X', not 'X was built'.
- Don't hide verbs as nouns ending in -ment, -tion, -sion, -ance. (digital.gov (U.S. General Services Administration; successor to plainlanguage.gov), n.d.; high)
  - Takeaway: Same signal Reinhart found in LLM text; checker can count -tion/-ment words per sentence.
- Prefer present tense, shorter words and short sections. **"The present tense makes your writing simpler, more direct, and more forceful."** (digital.gov (U.S. General Services Administration; successor to plainlanguage.gov), n.d.; high)
- Avoid formal long words and words ending in -ion and -ment. **Example: "buy" not "purchase"** (GOV.UK (UK Government Digital Service), n.d.; high)
  - Takeaway: Use short everyday words.
- Flesch Reading Ease target for most standard documents is 60 to 70. **FRE = 206.835 - (1.015 x ASL) - (84.6 x ASW); target 60-70** (Microsoft Support, n.d.; high)
  - Takeaway: Checker target: Flesch Reading Ease >= 60.
- Split sentences longer than 25 words. **"Try to split up sentences that are over 25 words long."** (GOV.UK (UK Government Digital Service), n.d.; high)
  - Takeaway: Checker: flag any sentence over 25 words.
- Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level target for most documents is about grade 7 to 8. **FKGL = (0.39 x ASL) + (11.8 x ASW) - 15.59; recommended about 7.0-8.0** (Microsoft Support, n.d.; high)
  - Takeaway: Checker target: grade level <= 8 (tech terms may push it up; cap ~9).
- Fancy language makes readers do extra work so the writer can seem cool, and simple writing exposes empty content. **"If you say nothing simply, it will be obvious to everyone, including you."** (paulgraham.com, 2021-03; medium)
  - Takeaway: If a plain rewrite of a sentence says nothing, delete it.
- Many readers aren't native English speakers, so hard words block them. **"A lot of your readers won't be native English speakers."** (paulgraham.com, 2021-03; medium)
  - Takeaway: Use common words; clients and recruiters are often non-native readers too.
- Experts and highly literate readers also prefer plain English. **"80% of people preferred sentences written in clear English - and the more complex the issue, the greater that preference" (research on legal documents, as cited)** (GOV.UK (UK Government Digital Service), n.d.; medium)
  - Takeaway: Technical clients don't want jargon-heavy prose either.
- Easier text gets deeper engagement and more readers finish it. **"The easier something is to read, the more deeply readers will engage with it."** (paulgraham.com, 2021-03; medium)
  - Takeaway: Short plain answers get read to the end by busy reviewers.

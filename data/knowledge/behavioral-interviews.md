# Behavioral interviews

Group: Interviews · Related vault file: `Job/Interview Prep/Prep Plan.md` · 4 sources, 10 findings

## Findings (strongest sources first)

- Google uses both behavioral ('Tell me about a time') and hypothetical ('What would you do if') questions in structured interviews. (Google re:Work, n.d.; high)
- Candidates may use AI to polish application materials and prep for interviews, but should write the first draft themselves and not invent experience. (Anthropic, 2025; high)
- The Amazon loop is 4-6 interviews of 45-60 minutes, including a Bar Raiser. **4-6 interviews, 45-60 minutes each** (About Amazon, n.d.; high)
  - Takeaway: Have enough distinct stories that you don't repeat one across the loop.
- Amazon recommends STAR answers tied to its 16 Leadership Principles, using 'I' statements and precise metrics. **responses of 2-3 minutes each** (About Amazon, n.d.; high)
- Google evaluates candidates on role-related knowledge, problem solving, and leadership (adjusted by level). (Google re:Work, n.d.; high)
- Google scores answers against rubrics that describe what a poor, borderline, solid and outstanding answer looks like for each attribute. (Google re:Work, n.d.; high)
  - Takeaway: Aim each story at a clear 'outstanding' bar: own actions, concrete result, reflection.
- After correcting for overcorrected range restriction, structured interviews ranked as the top-validity selection procedure for predicting job performance. **structured interview mean validity ~.42 vs ~.19 for unstructured (as reported in secondary summaries of the paper)** (Journal of Applied Psychology (via SMU Institutional Knowledge repository), 2022; medium)
  - Takeaway: Expect structured, rubric-scored behavioral rounds at serious employers; prepare consistent, evidence-backed stories.
- Most selection procedures that ranked high in Schmidt & Hunter (1998) still rank high, but their validity estimates drop by about .10-.20 once corrections are fixed. **Schmidt & Hunter 1998 had structured interviews at r = .51; Sackett et al. revised many estimates downward by .10-.20** (Journal of Applied Psychology (via SMU Institutional Knowledge repository), 2022; medium)
  - Takeaway: The interview format, not gut feel, carries the signal, so answering the question asked with specifics matters more than charm.
- Common behavioral prompts include teamwork on a project, solving complex problems, the project you're most proud of, and a time you failed. **"Tell me about a time you failed"** (MIT Career Advising & Professional Development, n.d.; high)
- MIT suggests weighting STAR answers heavily toward the Action you personally took. **Situation 20%, Task 10%, Action 60%, Result 10%** (MIT Career Advising & Professional Development, n.d.; high)

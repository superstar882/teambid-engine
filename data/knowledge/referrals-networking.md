# Referrals and networking

Group: Search · Related vault file: `Job/Playbook/Where to Find Jobs.md` · 8 sources, 15 findings

## Findings (strongest sources first)

- Referred workers performed especially well when working directly with the person who referred them. (National Bureau of Economic Research (Working Paper 21357), 2015-07; high)
  - Takeaway: Best referrals come from people who would actually work with you (future teammates).
- Across nine large firms (including high-tech), referred and non-referred applicants had similar skills, but referred hires quit less. (The Quarterly Journal of Economics (via RePEc/IDEAS), 2015; high)
  - Takeaway: Firms favor referrals because they stay; this is why companies pay referral bonuses and why employees will refer you.
- Field experiments on oDesk (now Upwork) found referrals carry real information about worker performance and persistence not visible in profiles. (National Bureau of Economic Research (Working Paper 21357), 2015-07; high)
  - Takeaway: On Upwork too, a referral or a client introduction signals quality that a new profile cannot.
- Referred workers produced more patents in high-tech and generated higher profit per worker, mostly through lower turnover and recruiting cost. (The Quarterly Journal of Economics (via RePEc/IDEAS), 2015; high)
  - Takeaway: Asking for a referral is not a favor-only ask; it often earns the referrer a bonus.
- The weak-tie effect was strongest in digital, software and AI-intensive industries. (MIT Sloan School of Management (press release on Rajkumar et al., Science 2022), 2022-09-15; high)
  - Takeaway: Software/AI is exactly where weak-tie networking pays off most.
- A randomized experiment on 20 million LinkedIn users over five years found moderately weak ties drive the most job mobility, not close friends. **20M users, ~600,000 job changes; inverted U-shape, moderately weak ties help most** (MIT Sloan School of Management (press release on Rajkumar et al., Science 2022), 2022-09-15; high)
- Most young professionals feel disconnected from people who could help their careers and worry about bothering them. **72% feel disconnected; 37% worry about bothering people (Censuswide, 18,050 respondents)** (WebWire (LinkedIn press release), 2026-09-03; high)
- Referrals are a tiny share of applications (38M applications, 93K jobs, 2021-2024). **About 1% of applications; fell from 2% (Q1 2021) to under 1%** (Ashby (Talent Trends Report), 2025-05-16; high)
  - Takeaway: Few applicants have a referral, so having one stands out.
- Even referral offer rates have declined as hiring got more selective. **Referral offer rate 12.1% (Q3 2021) to 7.3% (Q4 2024)** (Ashby (Talent Trends Report), 2025-05-16; high)
  - Takeaway: A referral gets you in the room; interview prep still decides it.
- Referral hires stay steady at about 17-18% regardless of how much inbound volume there is. **~17% referral share across 2021-2025** (Ashby (Talent Trends Report), 2025-10-17; high)
  - Takeaway: Roughly one in six startup hires is a referral; worth a steady weekly effort.
- Most mid-career professionals say they would help early-career contacts. **83% of Millennials and Gen X** (WebWire (LinkedIn press release), 2026-09-03; high)
  - Takeaway: Senior engineers are usually willing; ask clearly.
- Referred candidates reach the interview stage far more often than other applicants. **40% of referred candidates go from application to interview; 16% of interviewed referrals reach offer** (Ashby (Talent Trends Report), 2025-05-16; high)
  - Takeaway: A referral roughly turns a lottery ticket into a likely interview.
- Referrals were the third largest source of hires while not appearing among the top five application sources (each of which was at least 2.9% of applications). **Referrals: 11.6% of hires (2025)** (Greenhouse, 2026-03; medium)
  - Takeaway: A referral converts far better than a cold application; prioritize getting them for top-choice companies.
- LinkedIn found applicants with an inside connection were nearly 7x more likely to be hired than those without one. **nearly 7x (sample of 5M members per secondary reporting)** (WebWire (LinkedIn press release), 2026-09-03; medium)
  - Takeaway: Before applying, check for any first-degree connection at the company and message them.
- Cold referrals (from strangers) ranked among the least effective channels, unlike warm referrals. (interviewing.io, n.d.; medium)
  - Takeaway: Don't spam strangers for referrals; build a light relationship first.

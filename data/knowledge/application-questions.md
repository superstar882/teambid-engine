# Answering application questions

Group: Writing · Related vault file: `Job/Writing/04-question-playbook.md` · 15 sources, 30 findings

## Findings (strongest sources first)

- Replace adjectives about yourself with one concrete accomplishment that shows magnitude. **Swap claims like "Jordan is exceptionally dedicated" for an actual accomplishment.** (Y Combinator, n.d.; high)
- Marketing-speak is noise to readers; the test is whether a reader could reproduce your idea from your description. **Weak example: "We are going to transform the relationship between individuals and information."** (Y Combinator, n.d.; high)
  - Takeaway: Describe projects as concrete mechanisms (what goes in, what comes out), never 'innovative AI solutions'.
- Put the most important information in the first sentence, in the simplest possible terms, because readers skim about 100 applications a day. **"Whatever you have to say, give it to us right in the first sentence, in the simplest possible terms."** (Y Combinator, n.d.; high)
  - Takeaway: Answer every form question in sentence one, e.g. 'I built a multimodal RAG backend that's live in two products.'
- Cut hard before submitting; naming known weaknesses yourself reads stronger than hiding them. **"Print it out and take a red pen and cross out every word you don't need."** (Y Combinator, n.d.; high)
- Almost all recruiters have seen candidate deception, and most hiring managers have caught deceptive AI use. **91% of recruiters spotted deception; 65% of hiring managers caught applicants using AI deceptively** (Greenhouse, 2025-11; high)
- Employers are adding human checks to verify authenticity. **39% of hiring managers are doing more in-person interviews** (Greenhouse, 2025-11; high)
  - Takeaway: Expect follow-up questions on every written answer; keep answers true and explainable.
- Many candidates say AI made it harder to stand out. **26% say AI made standing out harder (45% of Gen Z)** (Greenhouse, 2025-07-15; high)
- Candidates admit to faking AI skills and work samples, so recruiters are primed to doubt AI claims. **32% claimed AI skills they don't have; 28% used AI to generate fake work samples** (Greenhouse, 2025-07-15; high)
  - Takeaway: Back every AI/RAG claim with a live link or a specific detail (Gemini via LangChain, ChromaDB, S3 cache).
- Hidden prompt injections in applications are common and get caught. **41% of U.S. job seekers admit using prompt injections; 22% of hiring managers who caught deception found hidden prompt injections in resumes** (Greenhouse, 2025-11; high)
  - Takeaway: Never use hidden text or keyword tricks.
- A large share of job seekers auto-apply with bots, which is why generic answers blend into a flood. **22% of job seekers use bots to apply automatically (31% of Gen Z)** (Greenhouse, 2025-07-15; high)
- Greenhouse's CEO calls mass AI applying plus AI screening an 'AI doom loop' that makes the system worse for both sides. **"We've called that the AI doom loop. Everyone's using their own AI to solve their own problem, but it's making the whole system worse." (Daniel Chait)** (Fortune, 2026-09-21; high)
  - Takeaway: Fewer, sharper, clearly human applications beat volume.
- You need to sound interested in actually doing the work, and a sentence or two of context is enough; no elaborate passion story is required. (Ask a Manager, 2019-07; medium)
  - Takeaway: For 'Why this role?', write 1-2 sentences tied to the actual work in the posting (e.g. retrieval quality, data pipelines).
- Green says she wants to hear candidates' own voice and writing, though well-edited AI help shouldn't be detectable. (Ask a Manager, 2026-09; medium)
- Green acknowledges the honest answer is often 'I need a job' and that manufactured enthusiasm isn't needed; genuine interest in the work plus having read the posting is usually sufficient. (Ask a Manager, 2019-07; medium)
- Reviewing a reader's AI-assisted letter, Green found its real flaw was summarizing work history and repeating the resume instead of adding something new. (Ask a Manager, 2026-09; medium)
  - Takeaway: Each answer should add a fact or story not already on the resume.
- Application volume per job rose sharply while recruiter headcount fell, so each answer gets less reading time. **Applications per job up 111% (2022-2025); recruiter headcount down 56%** (Fortune, 2026-09-21; medium)
  - Takeaway: Front-load the answer; assume a few seconds of attention.
- Core rule: don't use the letter to summarize the resume, add something new. **"don't just use your cover letter to summarize your resume — add something new"** (Ask a Manager, 2020-10; medium)
  - Takeaway: Use free-text boxes for the story behind a resume line, not the line itself.
- A strong 'why here' answer combines three things: why this company, why this position, and why you'll succeed in it. **"The strongest answers to 'why do you want to work here' are a combination of these three talking points."** (MIT Sloan Career Development Office (republished from Big Interview), 2023-04-05; high)
- Just over half of hiring managers accept AI for proofreading or drafting help, as long as the final product sounds human and reflects the person's own effort. **52% say AI for proofreading or drafting support is acceptable** (TopResume, 2025-05; medium)
- Lerner's goal is to start a conversation rather than request an interview, because the conversation is what proves you're worth interviewing. (interviewing.io, n.d.; medium)
- The company part should come from real research into the company's situation, not generic praise. (MIT Sloan Career Development Office (republished from Big Interview), 2023-04-05; medium)
  - Takeaway: Cite a real product, launch or engineering post from the company, not 'innovative culture'.
- A third of hiring managers say they spot AI-written resumes fast. **33.5% can spot an AI-generated resume in under twenty seconds** (TopResume, 2025-05; medium)
- About one in five hiring managers would reject a candidate with an AI-generated resume or cover letter, and another 20% treat heavy AI reliance as a red flag. **19.6% would reject; survey of 600 U.S. hiring managers, May 15-16, 2025 (Pollfish)** (TopResume, 2025-05; medium)
  - Takeaway: Never paste raw AI output into a form box.
- Managers report preferring an authentic but imperfect document over a polished AI one. **"a poorly written but authentic resume over a perfectly polished AI-generated one"** (Resume.io, n.d.; medium)
  - Takeaway: A slightly loose, specific, human answer beats a smooth generic one.
- A long-time hiring manager wants less than a page, ideally a couple of paragraphs, covering why this company and role, how the experience applies, and strengths not obvious from the CV. **"less than a page, ideally just a couple of paragraphs"** (Hacker News, 2025-07-10; medium)
  - Takeaway: Keep cover-letter boxes to 2 short paragraphs max.
- Green card holders are authorized to work now and generally do not need employer sponsorship, so the answers are Yes to authorization and No to sponsorship. (JobWizard, n.d.; medium)
  - Takeaway: Authorized to work in the US: Yes. Require sponsorship now or in future: No. Say 'permanent resident', never 'citizen'.
- Nearly half of surveyed US hiring managers say they dismiss resumes they identify as AI-generated. **49% of 3,000 hiring managers** (Resume.io, n.d.; medium)
  - Takeaway: Treat any AI-sounding answer as a rejection risk, not a time-saver.
- 'Are you legally authorized to work' asks about today, and is a separate question from future sponsorship needs. (JobWizard, n.d.; medium)
  - Takeaway: Answer each checkbox literally; don't add explanations unless there is a free-text box.
- A hiring manager said they ignore applications that don't say why this company, how the candidate would grow there, or even name the company. **"If you don't state why you want to work at the company... or even mention the company by name explicitly, I ignore the rest of the application."** (Hacker News, 2016-05-23; medium)
  - Takeaway: Always name the company and one specific reason in 'why us' answers.
- A self-identified hiring manager said a letter that rehashes the resume wastes everyone's time, and AI-written letters add little because they only repackage resume data. **"If the cover letter is a rehash of your resume, it's a waste of your time and mine" (hansvm)** (Hacker News, 2024-12-28; medium)
  - Takeaway: Use text boxes for context the resume can't show: why a side project exists, a pivot, a tradeoff.

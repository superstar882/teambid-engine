# Cover letters

Group: Offers · Related vault file: `Job/Writing/README.md` · 4 sources, 9 findings

## Findings (strongest sources first)

- A third of hiring managers said they can spot an AI-generated resume in under 20 seconds, while 52% said AI for proofreading or drafting help is acceptable. **33.5% spot within 20 seconds; 52% accept AI drafting/proofreading** (TopResume, 2025-05; medium)
  - Takeaway: Using AI as an editor is fine; letting it write the whole thing is the risk.
- Tech hiring leaders quoted said engineers using AI to help write resumes or cover letters is fine as long as the content stays accurate. (IEEE-USA InSight, 2025-11-19; medium)
  - Takeaway: Tech employers are more relaxed about AI help than general hiring managers, but accuracy is non-negotiable.
- In the companion survey of 236 hiring professionals, 87% said they read cover letters and 81% valued tailored letters well above generic ones. **87% read; 81% prefer tailored** (ResumeGo, 2020; medium)
- In a Pollfish survey of 625 U.S. hiring managers, 83% said they read most cover letters and 94% said cover letters influence who they interview. **83% read most; 94% influential** (Resume Genius, 2026-01-20; medium)
  - Takeaway: When a cover letter is optional, a short targeted one still helps; vendor survey, so treat figures as directional.
- 72% of respondents said customizing a cover letter is important or very important. **72%** (Resume Genius, 2026-01-20; medium)
  - Takeaway: A generic letter adds little; tailor it or skip it.
- In a field experiment with 7,287 applications, tailored cover letters got a much higher callback rate than applications with no cover letter. **53% higher callback rate (tailored vs none); search results reported 16.4% tailored, 12.5% generic, 10.7% none** (ResumeGo, 2020; medium)
  - Takeaway: Tailoring is what produces the lift; a generic letter barely beats none.
- Cover letters mattered more to medium and large companies (100+ employees) than to small businesses. (Resume Genius, 2026-01-20; medium)
  - Takeaway: At a tiny startup reached through an agency, a cover letter matters less than the recruiter's pitch and a direct note to the founder.
- In a May 2025 Pollfish survey of 600 U.S. hiring managers, about one in five said they would reject a candidate with an AI-generated resume or cover letter. **19.6%** (TopResume, 2025-05; medium)
  - Takeaway: An obviously AI-written letter can cost the interview; write in a personal voice with specific facts.
- A Korn Ferry source estimated that roughly 5% to 10% of job offers have to be rescinded because of resume falsehoods. **"5% to 10% of job offers"** (IEEE-USA InSight, 2025-11-19; low)
  - Takeaway: Every claim must be verifiable; inflated claims can cost an offer later.

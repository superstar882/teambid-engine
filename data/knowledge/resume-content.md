# Resume content and bullets

Group: Resume · Related vault file: `Job/Resume/rules/03-content-and-bullets.md` · 5 sources, 8 findings

## Findings (strongest sources first)

- Jobseekers with 99%+ correctly spelled words were hired nearly three times as often as those under 90%. **~3x** (MIT Sloan School of Management, 2023-04-26; high)
  - Takeaway: Zero typos, especially in tool names and numbers.
- Harvard advises resume language that is specific, active and written to express rather than impress, using strong action verbs. **"Specific rather than general, Active rather than passive, Written to express not impress"** (Harvard University, Mignone Center for Career Success, 2024-07; high)
  - Takeaway: Plain specific verbs (built, cut, moved) beat buzzwords like 'spearheaded' or 'leveraged'.
- MIT recommends action verb + what you did + quantified result, and focusing on accomplishments instead of responsibilities. **"Including a quantitative result makes your accomplishments more concrete and compelling."** (MIT Career Advising & Professional Development (CAPD), 2025; high)
- Google's former head of People Operations recommends writing each accomplishment as 'Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z]'. **"Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z]"** (LinkedIn, 2014-09-29; high)
- Top listed mistakes include spelling/grammar errors, passive language, poor organization and failing to show results. (Harvard University, Mignone Center for Career Success, 2024-07; high)
  - Takeaway: Every bullet should show an outcome, not a duty.
- Bullets should start with an active verb, include a number, give a baseline for comparison, and say how the result was achieved; this applies even to entry-level candidates. (LinkedIn, 2014-09-29; high)
  - Takeaway: Always give the before/after baseline (1 hour to under 15 minutes), not just the end number.
- Bock lists concise, error-free, legible and honest as basic resume requirements. (LinkedIn, 2014-09-29; medium)
- The author shares a free Google Docs/Word resume template from their book, which collected input from hiring managers and recruiters at large tech firms. (X (Twitter), n.d.; medium)
  - Takeaway: A simple single-column tech template is the norm for SWE roles.

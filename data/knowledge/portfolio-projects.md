# Portfolios and side projects

Group: Market · Related vault file: `Job/Resume/rules/05-ai-engineer-resume.md` · 6 sources, 6 findings

## Findings (strongest sources first)

- LLM side projects are now extremely common on GitHub, so a plain LLM demo repo is not distinctive. **~1.1M public repos import LLM SDKs (+178% YoY); ~4.3M AI-related repos** (GitHub, 2025-10-28; high)
- Many CS students already list AI competencies on resumes, so listing AI alone no longer differentiates. **45% highlight AI competencies on resumes; 42% use generative AI daily** (Handshake, 2025-08-14; high)
  - Takeaway: Show live AI products with links, not just 'AI' as a keyword.
- Despite 1,000+ applicants per role being common, many companies hire most engineers through outreach and referrals rather than inbound applications. **1,000+ candidates for a single role is not uncommon** (The Pragmatic Engineer, 2025-09; medium)
- SignalFire advises early-career engineers to build visible proof through open source, freelance projects, and skill at debugging AI-generated code. **"debugging messy, machine-generated code may be your superpower"** (SignalFire, 2025-05-20; medium)
  - Takeaway: Freelance Upwork work and live products are the kind of proof the report recommends.
- Most hiring managers say the flood of AI-enhanced applications makes it harder to verify candidate skills. **65% of hiring managers** (Robert Half (via PR Newswire), 2026-03; medium)
  - Takeaway: Verifiable proof (live links, demos) cuts through when resumes all look polished.
- Developers report hiring assessments don't match real work and prefer practical challenges, which favors candidates who can show real projects. **78% say assessments don't align with real work; 66% prefer practical challenges** (HackerRank, 2025; medium)

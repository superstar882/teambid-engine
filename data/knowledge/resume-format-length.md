# Resume format and length

Group: Resume · Related vault file: `Job/Resume/rules/02-structure-and-format.md` · 4 sources, 8 findings

## Findings (strongest sources first)

- Aim for one page (two max), keep margins at least 0.5 inch (ideally 0.75) and do not shrink the font to fit more. **Margins min 0.5", ideally 0.75"** (MIT Career Advising & Professional Development (CAPD), 2025; high)
  - Takeaway: Cut content before shrinking font or margins on the 1-page resume.
- Avoid personal pronouns, narrative style, photos, age or gender info, references, and keep formatting consistent so it translates to PDF. (Harvard University, Mignone Center for Career Success, 2024-07; high)
  - Takeaway: No photo, no 'I', consistent bold/italics; check the PDF export.
- The two-page preference was weakest for entry-level roles (1.4x) and strongest for managerial roles (2.9x). **Entry 1.4x, mid 2.6x, managerial 2.9x** (ResumeGo, 2018; medium)
  - Takeaway: As an early-career engineer, a dense one page is fine; go to two only if content is strong and relevant.
- In a hiring simulation with 482 recruiting professionals reviewing 7,712 resumes, two-page resumes were preferred 2.3x as often overall. **2.3x overall; 482 participants; 7,712 resumes** (ResumeGo, 2018; medium)
  - Takeaway: Two pages is not fatal, but the edge shrinks a lot at entry level.
- Simple layouts with clear sections, bold headings and bulleted accomplishments following an F/E reading pattern performed best; crowded layouts, multiple columns, long paragraphs and missing titles performed worst. (HR Dive, 2018-11-08; medium)
  - Takeaway: Use one column, clear headings, bullets, and visible job titles.
- Ladders' eye-tracking study found recruiters spend an average of 7.4 seconds on an initial resume screen, up from 6 seconds in 2012. **7.4 seconds** (HR Dive, 2018-11-08; medium)
- The study used about 30 recruiters (per other coverage), a small sample, and was run by a job-board company, so treat the exact number as indicative. **~30 recruiters** (HR Dive, 2018-11-08; low)
- Reviewers spent about 2m24s on one-page vs 4m05s on two-page resumes in the simulation (not a real screening setting). **2:24 vs 4:05** (ResumeGo, 2018; low)

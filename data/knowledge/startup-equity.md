# Startup equity

Group: Offers · Related vault file: `Job/Playbook/Negotiation.md` · 7 sources, 14 findings

## Findings (strongest sources first)

- Investors hold preferred stock with liquidation preferences and governance rights that common holders lack. (Andreessen Horowitz (a16z), 2016-08-24; high)
  - Takeaway: In a modest exit, preferred is paid first, so common equity can be worth much less than its percentage suggests.
- A 409A must be redone at least every 12 months, and after significant events, to keep IRS safe harbor. **every 12 months** (Holloway, 2022-09-12; high)
  - Takeaway: Ask the date of the last 409A; a stale or missing one is a diligence gap.
- New funding rounds dilute existing holders; in the article's example 100 options fall from 1.0% to 0.5% when the fully diluted count doubles. **"owning 0.09% of a $1 billion company is better than owning 0.1% of a $500 million company"** (Andreessen Horowitz (a16z), 2016-08-24; high)
  - Takeaway: Expect a founding-team percentage to shrink with each raise; model at least two future rounds.
- Candidates should ask for the most recent 409A valuation, whether early exercise is allowed, and whether they must exercise within 90 days of leaving. **"Am I required to exercise my options within 90 days after I leave or am terminated?"** (Holloway, 2022-09-12; high)
  - Takeaway: An internally funded startup may not have a 409A yet; ask how the strike price was set.
- The 409A valuation sets the fair market value used for option strike prices and is usually far below the price investors paid for preferred stock. **"often, it might be only a third or less of the preferred stock price"** (Holloway, 2022-09-12; high)
  - Takeaway: Don't value options at a headline investor valuation; common stock and preferred are different.
- Candidates should ask what percentage of the company their shares represent and whether that was computed on outstanding or fully diluted shares. **"What percentage of the company do the shares represent?"** (Holloway, 2022-09-12; high)
- Extending the exercise window past 90 days converts ISOs into non-qualified options, losing ISO tax treatment. (Andreessen Horowitz (a16z), 2016-07-26; high)
  - Takeaway: Trade-off to understand before negotiating a longer window.
- Ownership should be measured against the fully diluted share count, which includes options, warrants and unissued pool shares. (Andreessen Horowitz (a16z), 2016-08-24; high)
- The standard 90-day post-employment exercise window favors employees who have cash to exercise, so a16z recommends longer windows such as 10 years. **"pits cash-rich employees against cash-poor ones"** (Andreessen Horowitz (a16z), 2016-07-26; high)
  - Takeaway: Ask for an extended post-termination exercise window; it can be worth more than a slightly larger grant.
- Early employees can use early exercise plus an 83(b) election for tax advantages while the strike price is low. (Andreessen Horowitz (a16z), 2016-07-26; medium)
  - Takeaway: At a very early company with a low strike, early exercise and an 83(b) may be cheap; confirm with a tax advisor.
- At startups valued $1M-$10M, median initial equity grants to AI/ML engineers rose 64% over two years. **+64%** (Carta, 2025; medium)
  - Takeaway: Very early AI startups are paying up in equity for AI engineers, which supports asking for a larger grant.
- Over the past two years the median equity grant to the first engineer at a tech startup on Carta was 1.54%, with a wide interquartile range. **median 1.54%; 25th-75th percentile 0.61% to 3.5%** (Carta, 2025-12; medium)
  - Takeaway: A real founding-engineer offer below about 0.5% would sit in the bottom quartile; 1-2% is median territory. (Page returned 403; figures from search result text.)
- Equity falls fast for later engineers, with the median dropping to 0.61% within a couple of hires. **1.54% down to 0.61% after 2 hires** (Carta, 2025-12; medium)
- The guide cites survey ranges of up to 2-3% for hire #1, 1-2% for hires 2-5, and 0.2-0.33% for junior engineers post-Series A. **Hire #1: up to 2-3%; hires #2-5: up to 1-2%; junior engineer post-Series A: 0.2-0.33%** (Holloway, 2022-09-12; medium)
  - Takeaway: Older data (Leo Polovets survey) but consistent with Carta medians.
